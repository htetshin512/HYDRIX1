01 / THE DEFINITION

What Is Drought?

Drought is a prolonged period of unusually dry conditions when an area receives less rainfall than is normal for its climate. As the shortage continues, soil moisture may decline, streamflow can decrease, and reservoirs or groundwater may take longer to replenish.

A drought is different from a single hot day or a short period without rain. It is assessed over time by comparing rainfall and related conditions with what is typical for that location. Its severity depends on factors such as duration, temperature, water demand and existing water reserves.

Important: Hot weather can intensify drought by increasing water loss, but heat alone does not automatically mean a drought is occurring.
Develops Over Time Its effects can build up over weeks, months or longer

Recovery Can Be Slow Water supplies may remain low after rainfall returns
02 / CLASSIFICATION

The Four Types of Drought

Drought can be measured in different ways. These four types help explain how shortages affect rainfall, farming, water resources and the needs of communities.

01 / RAINFALL

Meteorological Drought

Occurs when a region receives substantially less rainfall than usual over an extended period. It is often an early indication that drought conditions are developing.

For Example A rainy season brings much less rain than expected
02 / FARMING

Agricultural Drought

Develops when there is not enough moisture in the soil to support crops and vegetation. Farmers may experience poor crop growth, reduced yields and greater irrigation needs.

For Example Young crops struggle because the soil is too dry
03 / WATER RESOURCES

Hydrological Drought

Happens when rivers, lakes, reservoirs or groundwater remain at unusually low levels. It may develop after rainfall shortages and can take longer to recover.

For Example A reservoir remains low following a weak rainy season
04 / COMMUNITIES

Socioeconomic Drought

Occurs when drought-related shortages affect the supply of goods and services, household needs, livelihoods and economic activities.

For Example Water shortages reduce farming income or food availability

How are they connected? A rainfall shortage can reduce soil moisture, affect rivers and reservoirs, and eventually disrupt people's daily activities. However, these effects may overlap or occur in a different order.

03 / CAUSES AND CONTRIBUTING FACTORS

What Causes Drought?

A prolonged lack of rainfall is a main cause of drought. Other natural conditions and human activities can increase water loss or make shortages harder for people to manage.

Below-Normal Rainfall

Changes in weather patterns can result in fewer rainy days, weaker seasonal rainfall or longer gaps between rain events.

Higher Temperatures

Hotter conditions can increase evaporation from soil, reservoirs and other water sources, making existing dry conditions more severe.

Climate Variability and Climate Change

Climate patterns influence seasonal rainfall. Long-term warming can increase water loss and intensify drought in many regions.

Unsustainable Water Use

Excessive groundwater extraction, inefficient irrigation and water waste can worsen shortages, especially during long dry periods.

04 / CONSEQUENCES

How Drought Affects Communities

Drought affects much more than the environment. Its consequences depend on water availability, community needs and how prepared people are.

Farming and Food Supply

Dry soil and limited irrigation can damage crops, reduce harvests and affect livestock. Lower agricultural production may also increase food costs.

FARMERS AND CONSUMERS

Household Water Access

Lower river, reservoir or groundwater levels may limit the amount available for drinking, cooking, cleaning and other daily needs.

HOUSEHOLDS

Health and Sanitation

Limited access to safe water can make hygiene and sanitation difficult. Using unsafe sources may increase the risk of water-related illnesses.

FAMILIES AND HEALTH SERVICES

Income and Local Businesses

Crop losses, higher spending on water and interruptions to water-dependent work can affect household income and local businesses.

WORKERS AND BUSINESSES

Wildlife and Ecosystems

Drought can place stress on rivers, wetlands, vegetation and wildlife. Less available water can damage habitats and affect biodiversity.

NATURAL ENVIRONMENTS

Education and Daily Life

Water shortages may disrupt schools and community facilities. Families may need to spend more time collecting water or managing limited supplies.

05 / MYANMAR CONTEXT

Drought in Myanmar

Myanmar's Central Dry Zone includes parts of Mandalay, Magway and Sagaing. Many communities depend on seasonal rainfall for agriculture and everyday water needs. Long dry periods can make reliable water access and proper water storage especially important.

The challenges vary between communities depending on their water sources, farming methods and access to infrastructure. Monitoring rainfall, maintaining local water facilities and following early warnings can help households and communities prepare.
