01 / THE REGIONAL CONTEXT

Understanding Myanmar's Central Dry Zone

Myanmar's Central Dry Zone lies in the interior of the country and includes parts of Mandalay, Magway and Sagaing Regions. It generally receives less rainfall than many other areas of Myanmar.

The Dry Zone is not a desert. Rainfall arrives mainly during the monsoon, while the remaining months are considerably drier. The amount and timing of rainfall vary between locations and from year to year.

Water availability depends on more than rainfall. Wells, reservoirs, rivers, irrigation systems, storage facilities and the needs of local communities all influence access to water.

02 / WATER AVAILABILITY

Why Water Shortages Happen

Different communities experience different water challenges. These three factors help explain why planning and water management matter.

01 / CLIMATE

Uneven Rainfall

Most rainfall occurs during the monsoon months. Delayed rain, long dry spells or unusually weak rainfall can reduce soil moisture and make agricultural planning more difficult.

02 / INFRASTRUCTURE

Water Supply & Storage

Communities depend on water sources such as wells, ponds, rivers and reservoirs. Poor maintenance, inadequate storage and limited distribution systems can worsen shortages.

03 / DEMAND

Community Water Needs

Households, schools, farms and businesses use water for different purposes. Water shortages can become more serious when demand rises or available supplies are not managed carefully.

03 / REGIONAL EXPLORER

Exploring Towns in Myanmar's Central Dry Zone

Myanmar's Central Dry Zone covers parts of Sagaing, Mandalay and Magway Regions. Although these areas share relatively dry climatic conditions, their water resources, agricultural activities and community needs are not exactly the same. Explore nine selected towns to understand their geography, water requirements, possible challenges and practical preparedness measures.

01
NORTHERN & CENTRAL MYANMAR

Sagaing Region

Monywa · Shwebo · Salingyi

03 TOWNS

Monywa is situated beside the Chindwin River in Sagaing Region. The river is an important geographical feature and a potential source of water for surrounding communities. However, being close to a river does not always guarantee reliable access to clean drinking water. Households, agricultural activities and local businesses all require water throughout the year. During prolonged dry periods, the availability of safe water, irrigation supplies and functioning storage facilities becomes especially important.

Water NeedsWater is needed for drinking, cooking, sanitation, farming and irrigation. Local households and agricultural communities depend on reliable supplies to maintain daily activities and support crop production.
Potential ChallengesIrregular seasonal rainfall, limited access to treated water, changes in water availability and poorly maintained distribution systems may create difficulties during extended dry periods.
PreparednessCommunities can inspect water facilities, protect stored drinking water, repair leaks and improve irrigation efficiency. Monitoring local conditions can also help residents prepare before shortages become serious.

Shwebo is an important agricultural town in Sagaing Region. Farming activities in the surrounding areas depend on seasonal rainfall and access to suitable irrigation supplies. Agricultural water needs can change throughout the growing season, particularly when crops require additional moisture. When rainfall arrives later than expected or dry periods continue for longer, farmers may need to adjust planting activities and water use. Maintaining reliable irrigation facilities and managing available water carefully can support agricultural livelihoods.

Water NeedsCrop cultivation, irrigation, livestock care and household activities require dependable supplies. Agricultural water demand may increase during growing periods when natural rainfall is insufficient.
Potential ChallengesDelayed monsoon rainfall, reduced soil moisture, water loss from irrigation systems and limited storage capacity can affect agricultural planning and increase pressure on local resources.
PreparednessFarmers can maintain irrigation channels, monitor seasonal weather information and use suitable water-saving methods. Communities can also protect storage facilities and encourage careful water management.

Salingyi is located in lower Sagaing Region, within the wider Central Myanmar Dry Zone area. Rural households and agricultural communities may depend on combinations of wells, ponds, stored rainwater and other local water sources. The reliability of these supplies can change with seasonal rainfall, infrastructure conditions and water demand. Access to safe drinking water is particularly important because limited supplies may encourage the use of sources that have not been properly protected or treated.

Water NeedsHouseholds require water for drinking, hygiene, cooking and cleaning, while surrounding agricultural activities also need adequate supplies. Safe storage is important when water is collected from shared sources.
Potential ChallengesSome communities may face limited storage, unreliable local sources or difficulties maintaining wells and ponds. Water contamination can become an additional concern when supplies are insufficient.
PreparednessRegular inspections of wells and storage tanks, clean covered containers and protection of community water sources can improve readiness. Residents can also plan essential household water requirements in advance.
02
CENTRAL DRY ZONE

Mandalay Region

Myingyan · Nyaung-U · Kyaukpadaung

03 TOWNS

Myingyan is located in Mandalay Region and is one of the towns associated with Myanmar's Central Dry Zone. Seasonal rainfall plays an important role in local agricultural activities, while wells, reservoirs and irrigation facilities can provide additional water where available. Changes in rainfall patterns may influence crop growth, soil moisture and household supplies. Effective water management is important because agricultural and domestic activities may compete for limited resources during prolonged dry periods.

Water NeedsWater is required for rain-fed farming, irrigated agriculture, livestock and daily household activities. Farmers may need additional irrigation when rainfall does not provide enough moisture for crops.
Potential ChallengesIrregular rainfall, reduced soil moisture, inefficient irrigation and limited water storage could make agricultural planning more difficult and increase pressure on community supplies.
PreparednessMaintaining storage systems, conserving soil moisture and using appropriate irrigation methods can help reduce unnecessary water loss. Households should also prepare safe water supplies for essential needs.

Nyaung-U is located near the historic Bagan area in Mandalay Region. The surrounding landscape experiences seasonal changes in rainfall, with wetter monsoon months and a comparatively dry period. Water is needed not only by households and agricultural communities but also by local businesses, accommodation facilities and public services. Because different activities require water throughout the year, reliable distribution and storage systems are important for managing demand during drier months.

Water NeedsResidents, farms, schools, businesses and visitor facilities require water for drinking, hygiene, cleaning and daily operations. These different needs place demands on available local supplies.
Potential ChallengesSeasonal water demand, leaking pipes, insufficient storage and limited access to dependable sources may create difficulties when rainfall remains low for an extended period.
PreparednessHouseholds and businesses can repair leaks, use water-saving equipment and maintain clean storage facilities. Public awareness campaigns can also encourage responsible water consumption.

Kyaukpadaung is situated near Mount Popa in Mandalay Region. Its surroundings include agricultural communities that depend on seasonal weather conditions and locally available water sources. Rainfall patterns are important for crop production, while wells, ponds and reservoirs may help supply water when rain is limited. Local geography can also influence water availability, meaning that different communities may experience different conditions even within the same township.

Water NeedsAgriculture, livestock, household activities and community facilities require water throughout the year. Farmers depend on sufficient soil moisture and appropriate supplies during important growing periods.
Potential ChallengesUnpredictable rainfall, insufficient water storage, declining soil moisture and poorly maintained sources may affect farming activities and access to everyday water supplies.
PreparednessCommunities can protect ponds and reservoirs, inspect wells and encourage efficient water use. Farmers may also improve soil moisture conservation and plan agricultural activities according to seasonal conditions.
03
CENTRAL & WESTERN MYANMAR

Magway Region

Pakokku · Chauk · Magway

03 TOWNS

Pakokku is located in northern Magway Region, near the Ayeyarwady River. Although the river is a significant geographical water resource, nearby communities still need suitable systems for obtaining, treating and distributing safe water. Households, agricultural activities and local businesses have different water requirements. During prolonged dry periods, dependable storage facilities and efficient use of available supplies can help communities manage increasing pressure on water resources.

Water NeedsWater supports household activities, crop production, irrigation, sanitation and local businesses. Safe drinking water and dependable community distribution systems are particularly important for daily life.
Potential ChallengesUnequal access to reliable supplies, damaged infrastructure, water quality concerns and increased seasonal demand may create challenges despite the town's proximity to a major river.
PreparednessProtecting drinking-water sources, maintaining storage tanks and repairing leaks can reduce risks. Communities can also encourage responsible water use and identify alternative supplies where practical.

Chauk is situated in Magway Region and is part of the broader Central Myanmar Dry Zone. The area experiences seasonal differences between wetter monsoon months and relatively dry periods. Water is needed for homes, workplaces and agricultural activities throughout the year. If dry conditions continue longer than usual, communities may need to rely more carefully on available storage and dependable water sources. Preparing before shortages develop can help protect essential household and community activities.

Water NeedsHouseholds need water for drinking, cooking, cleaning and sanitation, while workplaces and agricultural communities also require suitable supplies for their daily activities.
Potential ChallengesLong dry spells, inefficient water use, damaged storage facilities and limited alternative sources may increase difficulties when water availability becomes restricted.
PreparednessResidents can inspect wells and tanks, repair leaking taps and prepare plans for essential water use. Sharing conservation advice can also help households reduce unnecessary consumption.

Magway is an important urban centre in central Myanmar, situated near the Ayeyarwady River. The town contains households, educational institutions, healthcare facilities, businesses and surrounding agricultural communities that require water for different purposes. Reliable infrastructure is important because interruptions to water supplies may affect hygiene, public services and economic activities. Effective planning, conservation and maintenance can help improve preparedness during prolonged periods of limited rainfall.

Water NeedsWater is essential for homes, schools, hospitals, businesses, public sanitation and agricultural activities. Community facilities need dependable supplies to continue providing important services.
Potential ChallengesHigh water demand, ageing or damaged distribution systems, supply interruptions and insufficient storage may create difficulties during unusually dry conditions.
PreparednessCommunities can maintain distribution systems, repair leaks and protect drinking-water quality. Schools and healthcare facilities can also prepare essential water arrangements for possible supply disruptions.
UNDERSTANDING REGIONAL DIFFERENCES

Why Do Water Challenges Differ Between Towns?

Although these nine towns are located within the broader Central Dry Zone, their water availability is influenced by different geographical and human factors. Towns near major rivers may have access to significant water resources, while other communities depend more heavily on wells, reservoirs, ponds and seasonal rainfall.

Agriculture also affects water demand. Farming communities may require large amounts of water for irrigation, especially when rainfall is limited. Urban areas need reliable supplies for households, businesses, schools and healthcare facilities.

Understanding these differences helps communities identify suitable water conservation strategies and prepare for possible shortages before they become more serious.

These are educational reference profiles describing geographical context, possible water challenges and preparedness measures. They are not live drought reports or verified assessments of current water conditions in individual towns.

05 / COMMUNITY RESILIENCE

Water Shortage Challenges and Preparedness

Water shortages can affect farming, household activities, health, education and local businesses. Communities with limited water storage or unreliable water sources may face greater difficulties during prolonged dry periods.

Preparing before a drought becomes severe is important. By improving water management, protecting drinking-water supplies, maintaining infrastructure and raising public awareness, communities can reduce their vulnerability and protect essential daily activities.

Reliable Water Access

Communities should identify available water sources, including wells, ponds, reservoirs and public water systems. Regular inspections can help detect damage, declining supplies and possible problems before shortages become severe.

Safe Drinking Water

Limited water supplies may increase dependence on unsafe sources. Households should protect stored drinking water from contamination, use clean and covered containers, and follow reliable guidance on appropriate water treatment.

Agricultural Planning

Farmers can prepare by considering seasonal rainfall, crop water requirements and available irrigation supplies. Appropriate irrigation methods, soil moisture conservation and careful planting schedules can help reduce unnecessary water use.

Schools and Community Facilities

Schools, clinics and community buildings need water for drinking, hygiene and sanitation. Maintaining storage systems, reducing waste and preparing alternative supply arrangements can help essential services continue during shortages.

Water Conservation and Maintenance

Damaged pipes, leaking taps and poorly maintained tanks can waste valuable water. Regular maintenance, efficient water use and suitable rainwater harvesting can help communities manage existing supplies more effectively.

Community Awareness

Public awareness helps families understand the risks of water shortages and the importance of preparation. Schools and community groups can share information about water-saving habits, safe storage, local conditions and practical preparedness activities.
